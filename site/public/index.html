Three ways poisoned content reaches your model

  1. T1

    Edits around the pipeline

    Someone writes to the vector store directly, or changes a chunk after it was indexed.

    Signatures catch it. 125 of 125 tampered chunks were dropped in testing.

  2. T2

    Low-trust sources

    Uploads, scraped pages and shared drives carry text aimed at the model.

    Trust tiers stop silent hits. 0 of 88 poisoned chunks were served without a warning.

  3. T3

    A compromised trusted page

    An approved wiki page is edited. Signing can't help, because the page really is from your wiki.

    Delta scoring isolates the edit. This is the open problem, and where our research is focused.

How it works

  1. sign

    Sign at ingestion

    Each chunk's hash and source metadata are signed with Ed25519 and kept in a provenance ledger, with key revocation.

  2. diff

    Score what changed

    When a page is re-ingested, only the new paragraphs or sentences are scored, so a short payload isn't diluted by the legitimate page around it.

  3. detect

    Detect locally

    An injection classifier and an embedding-outlier model score content. Nothing leaves your network.

  4. filter

    Filter at retrieval

    Unverifiable chunks are dropped. Suspicious trusted content is served with a warning and queued for review, never silently deleted. A lockdown switch serves only clean, verified chunks during an incident.

Questions

Does my data leave my network?

No. The SDK, the detectors and the provenance ledger run in your environment. The hosted dashboard stores chunk IDs, scores and provenance metadata, not text, unless you turn on previews.

Isn't a prompt-injection classifier enough?

On our security corpus, an off-the-shelf classifier separated attacks from normal logs and incident reports barely better than chance. Provenance, delta scoring and calibration on your own content are what make detection usable.

Will it delete legitimate content?

Never on a detector score alone for trusted sources. Suspicious trusted content is served with a warning and goes to a human review queue.

Which stacks are supported?

Python RAG pipelines. LangChain, LlamaIndex, ChromaDB and pgvector adapters are planned for the first release.
