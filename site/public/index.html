threat model

Three ways poisoned content reaches your model

Direct edits to the index

Someone writes to the vector store, or changes a chunk after it was indexed.

Signatures catch it. 125 of 125 tampered chunks were dropped in testing.

Untrusted sources

Uploads, scraped pages and shared drives carry text aimed at the model.

Trust tiers stop silent hits. 0 of 88 poisoned chunks were served without a warning.

A compromised trusted page

An approved wiki page is edited. Signing can't help, because the page really is from your wiki.

Delta scoring isolates the edit, and a flagged edit waits for review. With our fine-tuned classifier, 94–96% of edits that plant instructions were flagged on public docs it never saw.

how it works

Four checks between your index and your model

Access control decides who can edit a page. Query-time guardrails react once content is already in the index. RAG Shield sits between the two: it judges each edit before it is indexed, and replaces neither.

Sign at ingestion

Each chunk's hash, source, version and editor are signed with Ed25519 and kept in a provenance ledger, so every answer traces back to who changed what.

Score what changed

A re-ingested page is compared with its last signed version, and only what the edit adds is scored: instructions aimed at the assistant, off-topic text, hidden characters, and links, emails or amounts swapped for new ones.

Detect locally

Our fine-tuned injection classifier and an embedding-outlier model run in your environment, on a CPU.

Filter at retrieval

Unverified chunks are dropped. A flagged edit waits for review with a reason in plain words, such as “points a link to a site the page didn’t link to before”, while the previous version serves.

open source

A few lines in your pipeline

pythonapache-2.0
from ragshield.shield import Shield
from ragshield.provenance import Ledger, Signer
from ragshield.index import Embedder, VectorIndex
from ragshield.detectors import InjectionDetector

shield = Shield(
    Ledger("provenance.db"), Signer.load_or_create("shield.pem"),
    Embedder(), VectorIndex(), injection=InjectionDetector(),
)

# sign and score; re-ingesting a page scores only what changed
shield.ingest("wiki/vpn-keys", page_text,
              source_id="confluence", trust_tier="high")

# verified, filtered and re-ranked by trust
for hit in shield.retrieve("rotate the VPN signing key", k=5):
    print(hit["chunk_id"], hit["trust"]["action"])

  • Three trust tiers: high, medium and low
  • Every drop and review goes to an audit trail
  • LangChain and LlamaIndex retrievers, and ChromaDB and pgvector adapters
  • ragshield-report: a read-only scan of your documents that shows what would be flagged

faq

Questions

Does my data leave my network?

No. The SDK, the detectors and the provenance ledger run in your environment. The hosted dashboard stores chunk IDs, scores and provenance metadata, not text, unless you turn on previews.

Isn't a prompt-injection classifier enough?

On our security corpus, an off-the-shelf classifier separated attacks from normal logs and incident reports barely better than chance (AUROC 0.54). On public how-to docs, delta scoring with that classifier flagged 46% of edits that plant instructions; with our fine-tuned classifier, 94–96%. Our first fine-tune scored nearly half of how-to docs as attacks, so we retrained it and retested on docs it had never seen. Provenance, delta scoring and testing on content like your own are what make detection usable.

Will it delete legitimate content?

Never on a detector score alone for trusted sources. A flagged edit to a trusted page waits for a reviewer while the previous version keeps serving, and other suspicious trusted content is served with a warning.

What happens during an incident?

A lockdown switch serves only verified chunks with no warnings at all while you investigate.

Which stacks are supported?

Python RAG pipelines. The SDK includes LangChain and LlamaIndex retrievers, and adapters for ChromaDB and pgvector.
