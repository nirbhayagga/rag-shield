Provenance and poisoning detection for retrieval-augmented AI

Know where every retrieved chunk came from, and whether to trust it.

Your assistant trusts whatever sits in its index, and anyone who can edit a wiki page or ticket can put text there. RAG Shield signs every chunk at ingestion, isolates what each edit to a trusted page adds, and checks every chunk at retrieval, before your model sees it.

threat model

Three ways poisoned content reaches your model

Direct edits to the index

Someone writes to the vector store, or changes a chunk after it was indexed.

Signatures catch it. 125 of 125 tampered chunks were dropped in testing.

Untrusted sources

Uploads, scraped pages and shared drives carry text aimed at the model.

Trust tiers stop silent hits. 0 of 88 poisoned chunks were served without a warning.

A compromised trusted page

An approved wiki page is edited. Signing can't help, because the page really is from your wiki.

Delta scoring isolates the edit. With our fine-tuned classifier, 93–95% of edits that plant instructions were flagged on public docs it never saw.

how it works

Four checks between your index and your model

Sign at ingestion

Each chunk's hash and source are signed with Ed25519 and kept in a provenance ledger.

Score what changed

A re-ingested page is compared with its last signed version, and only the new sentences are scored.

Detect locally

An injection classifier and an embedding-outlier model run in your environment.

Filter at retrieval

Unverified chunks are dropped. Suspicious trusted chunks are served with a warning and queued for review.

sdk preview

A few lines in your pipeline

pythonsdk preview
from ragshield.shield import Shield
from ragshield.provenance import Ledger, Signer
from ragshield.index import Embedder, VectorIndex
from ragshield.detectors import InjectionDetector

shield = Shield(
    Ledger("provenance.db"), Signer.load_or_create("shield.pem"),
    Embedder(), VectorIndex(), injection=InjectionDetector(),
)

# sign and score; re-ingesting a page scores only what changed
shield.ingest("wiki/vpn-keys", page_text,
              source_id="confluence", trust_tier="high")

# verified, filtered and re-ranked by trust
for hit in shield.retrieve("rotate the VPN signing key", k=5):
    print(hit["chunk_id"], hit["trust"]["action"])
  • Pure Python, runs on a CPU
  • Three trust tiers: high, medium and low
  • Every drop and review goes to an audit trail
  • LangChain, LlamaIndex, ChromaDB and pgvector adapters are planned for the first release

faq

Questions

Does my data leave my network?

No. The SDK, the detectors and the provenance ledger run in your environment. The hosted dashboard stores chunk IDs, scores and provenance metadata, not text, unless you turn on previews.

Isn't a prompt-injection classifier enough?

On our security corpus, an off-the-shelf classifier separated attacks from normal logs and incident reports barely better than chance (AUROC 0.54). On public how-to docs, delta scoring with that classifier flagged 46% of poisoned edits; with our fine-tuned classifier, 93–95%. Our first fine-tune scored nearly half of how-to docs as attacks, so we retrained it and retested on docs it had never seen. Provenance, delta scoring and testing on content like your own are what make detection usable.

Will it delete legitimate content?

Never on a detector score alone for trusted sources. Suspicious trusted content is served with a warning and goes to a human review queue.

What happens during an incident?

A lockdown switch serves only verified chunks with no warnings at all while you investigate.

Which stacks are supported?

Python RAG pipelines. LangChain, LlamaIndex, ChromaDB and pgvector adapters are planned for the first release.
